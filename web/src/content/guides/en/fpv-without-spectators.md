Most FPV pilots know it: the quad is barely in the air and someone with a dog
stops to watch. That is not just annoying – in the open category you have to
protect uninvolved people, and flying a 5-inch quad near people is simply
dangerous. The best spot is one where nobody comes past in the first place.

## How to recognise a quiet spot

**Far from paths.** People move along paths. A field 300 metres from the
nearest path is quieter than one right next to it – however large it is.

**Away from signposted routes.** Hiking trails and cycle routes with signposts
draw many times more people. An unremarkable farm track that is part of a
regional cycle route can be busy at weekends.

**No destinations nearby.** Car parks, viewpoints, barbecue sites, playgrounds
and inns are starting points for walks. Around them it is busier than the map
suggests.

**Far from residential areas.** Dog walkers, joggers and children start from
home. Distance to housing brings quiet – and from 250 g take-off mass, 150 m is
required anyway.

**The right time.** On Sunday afternoons recreational areas and forest edges are
full; on weekday mornings they are empty. Fields, on the other hand, are busy at
harvest time – with tractors instead of walkers.

## Quiet and still good to fly

An empty place is not automatically a good FPV spot. It also needs:

- **Open ground** for take-off, landing and a clear view.
- **Structure to fly around:** forest edges, tree rows and shorelines make
  freestyle interesting – with enough distance not to get stuck.
- **No power lines** nearby.

## The finder does exactly this

The [FPV Spot Finder](/en/app) rates every point in your area by these criteria:
distance to paths, signposted routes, destinations, residential areas and roads
– weighted by day and time – plus open ground, structure and power lines. Every
spot says **why** it scores well, such as "nearest path 420 m away", so you can
follow the rating before you set off.
