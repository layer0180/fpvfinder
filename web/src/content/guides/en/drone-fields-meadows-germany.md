Fields and meadows are the classic FPV spots: open, far from houses, few
people. In air law they are mostly unproblematic – **trouble starts on the
ground**: walking onto them, taking off and landing, and crashing into the
wheat. This guide separates the two.

> As of October 2026. Not legal advice. Access rights are a matter for the
> federal states and differ in detail.

## The airspace above the field

The flight itself follows the usual rules: open category, 120 m at most, an
observer next to you, and with a heavier quad without class label 150 m from
residential and recreational areas. A field or meadow is **not a restricted
zone of its own** under § 21h of the German Air Traffic Order. It only becomes
restricted when it lies in a geographical zone – a nature reserve, near an
airfield, along a federal road or railway. Check that on
[dipul.de](https://www.dipul.de/homepage/en/), as described in
[Geozones and the dipul map](/en/guides/geozones-dipul-map).

## May I walk onto the field?

The Federal Nature Conservation Act lets everyone enter the open countryside
**on paths and unused land** for recreation. Farmland in use is different:

- **Fields and meadows during the growing season** – roughly from sowing or
  growth until harvest or mowing – are off limits. A trampled cornfield or tall
  grass before the cut is real damage for the farmer.
- **Outside the growing season** – stubble, mown meadows, winter – access is
  allowed in many states.
- **Pastures with animals** and fenced land are not for walking onto.

Whether flying a drone still counts as "recreation" under the access rules is
handled differently by the states and is not clear-cut. You are safest with
**the consent of the owner or tenant**.

## Taking off and landing on someone else's land

Take off and land **from the path** where possible, or from land you have
permission for. Taking off, landing or retrieving your quad on someone else's
property without consent interferes with their property – the owner can send
you away and claim damages.

In practice:

- If you fly there regularly: **ask the farmer.** Many don't mind if you say
  when you come and look after the crop.
- Farm tracks are no car parks. Keep access free for tractors.
- After a crash into tall crops: retrieve it on the shortest way, don't search
  your way through the field. A GPS module or buzzer on the quad saves a lot of
  crop.

## What to watch over fields

- **Farm machinery and people.** Anyone working the field is an uninvolved
  person. Keep a wide berth in A3 and never fly over harvesters or sprayers.
- **Animals.** Livestock and wildlife are startled by drones. In spring,
  ground-nesting birds such as skylarks and lapwings breed on fields and
  meadows – circling low over them can breach the ban on disturbance in nature
  conservation law.
- **Power lines and wind turbines** often stand right in the fields. The
  [FPV Spot Finder](/en/app) warns about nearby power lines for every spot.
- **Paths with walkers and cyclists** along the edge – uninvolved people as
  well.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can a farmer forbid me to fly over his field?** He can forbid walking onto
it and taking off or landing on his land. For flying over at a sensible height,
the air law rules apply – a field is not a residential property. A friendly
conversation solves this better than any legal question.

**Is a harvested field a good spot?** Often, yes: open, soft for landing, no
crop to damage. Still check the geozones and the distance to the next
residential area.

**How do I find such areas?** The [FPV Spot Finder](/en/app) rates open land
around you and flags the access rules on farmland. Whether a field is being
worked right now, you only see on site.
