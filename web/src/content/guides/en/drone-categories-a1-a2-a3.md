The EU drone regulation sorts flights into three **categories**: *open*,
*specific* and *certified*. Hobby FPV almost always happens in the **open
category**, which is split into three **subcategories** – A1, A2 and A3 – that
mostly regulate how close you may get to people.

> As of October 2026. Not legal advice – the regulation and the German Federal
> Aviation Office (LBA) are authoritative.

## What applies across the open category

- At most **120 metres** above ground.
- Flights within **visual line of sight** – with FPV goggles only with an
  **observer** right next to you.
- **No flights over assemblies of people**, no dangerous goods, no dropping
  anything.
- At most **25 kg** take-off mass.

## The three subcategories

| | A1 | A2 | A3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Idea | over people (briefly, occasionally) | close to people | far from people |
| Typical drones | C0, C1, self-built below 250 g | C2 | C2, C3, C4, self-built below 25 kg |
| Distance to uninvolved people | no assemblies; with C1 avoid overflight | 30 m (5 m in low-speed mode) | do not endanger uninvolved people |
| Distance to residential, commercial, industrial, recreational areas | – | – | **150 m** |
| Proof | from 250 g: A1/A3 proof of competence | plus A2 certificate | from 250 g: A1/A3 proof of competence |

## What this means for your FPV quad

A typical FPV quad has **no class label** because it is self-built or put
together from parts. Since 1 January 2024:

- **Below 250 g** take-off mass (including battery): subcategory **A1**.
  Typical for tiny whoops and light 3-inch quads.
- **From 250 g up to 25 kg:** subcategory **A3** only – far from people and
  150 m from built-up and recreational areas. That covers most 5-inch freestyle
  and racing quads.
- EASA adds that **drones faster than 19 m/s** are limited to A3.

Because take-off mass counts, **weigh** your ready-to-fly quad with battery and
action cam.

## Registration, competence, insurance

- **Operator registration** with the LBA: required from 250 g **or** as soon as
  the drone has a camera – so always for FPV. The operator number goes visibly
  on the drone.
- **A1/A3 proof of competence:** required from 250 g, recommended below. Online
  training and an online test with the LBA; valid for five years. Steps and
  fees: [Drone licence in Germany](/en/guides/drone-license-germany-a1-a3-a2).
- **Liability insurance:** mandatory for all drones in Germany. Many personal
  liability policies exclude drones – check the terms.

## And the specific category?

Flying alone with goggles, over people or beyond visual line of sight leaves
the open category. That needs an operational authorisation or a standard
scenario in the **specific category** – usually too much effort for a hobby.
The exception are model flying associations with their own authorisation under
Article 16: as a member you fly by their rules.

## In practice: finding A3 spots

The 150-metre rule is why good A3 spots rarely sit right at the edge of town.
The [FPV Spot Finder](/en/app) blocks points closer than 150 m to residential
or recreational areas and shows for every spot how far the nearest residential
area is. More on choosing a place in
[Where can I fly FPV in Germany?](/en/guides/where-to-fly-fpv-germany).
