Officially there is no "drone licence" – people mean two certificates under the
EU drone regulation: the **EU proof of competency A1/A3** and the **EU remote
pilot certificate A2**. In Germany, both are issued by the Federal Aviation
Office (Luftfahrt-Bundesamt, LBA).

> As of October 2026. Not legal advice – the regulation and the LBA are binding.

## Do I need one for FPV?

- **FPV quads from 250 g** take-off mass (battery and camera included): **yes**,
  at least the A1/A3 proof of competency. That covers practically every 5-inch
  quad.
- **Below 250 g**, such as Tiny Whoops: not required, but worth it – the test
  is short, and the rules it covers apply to you anyway.
- **Registering as an operator** is a separate thing: it is required for every
  drone with a camera, so always for FPV, whatever the weight.

Which subcategory applies to your quad is explained in
[A1, A2, A3 explained](/en/guides/drone-categories-a1-a2-a3).

## A1/A3 proof of competency: step by step

1. **Create an account with the LBA.** You do not have to be registered as an
   operator to take the test.
2. **Work through the online training.** It is free and prepares you for
   exactly this exam.
3. **Online exam:** 40 multiple-choice questions from nine subject areas. You
   pass with at least **75 per cent** correct. Failed? You can repeat training
   and exam.
4. The certificate arrives by e-mail and is valid for **five years**.

Typical subjects: air safety, airspace restrictions, aviation regulation, human
performance limitations, operational procedures, privacy and data protection,
insurance, security and general knowledge about drones.

## A2 remote pilot certificate: the next step

The A2 certificate is for flying a **class C2** drone close to people. For
self-built FPV quads without a class label it brings **no extra rights** in the
open category – from 250 g they stay limited to A3. For most FPV pilots, A2 is
not a must.

The way there:

1. A1/A3 proof of competency.
2. **Practical self-training** under A3 conditions – in open country, away from
   people – and a declaration that you completed it.
3. **An additional theory exam** at a test centre designated by the LBA. Many
   centres combine it with practical training.
4. Application to the LBA. The A2 certificate is valid for five years as well.

## What does it cost?

The LBA charges **one-off fees** for registration and certificates under the
German aviation fee ordinance; the exact amount is in the fee notice in your
account. Notices sent so far are in the low double digits (the LBA quotes
30 euros for the A2 certificate). For A2, the test centre's own fee comes on
top.

## Renewing after five years

The certificates expire automatically after five years. You can renew them
once they have less than twelve months left – by taking the exam again or by a
refresher course. Renewal costs a fee again. Whoever earns a higher certificate
gets one common new expiry date for all categories on it.

## How old do I have to be?

Under the EU regulation the minimum age for remote pilots is **16**.
Exceptions: class C0 toy drones, **privately built drones below 250 g** – so
many Tiny Whoops – and flying under the direct supervision of a remote pilot
who meets all requirements. The LBA has a separate application form for
minors.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is the German certificate valid in other EU countries?** Yes, it is an EU
certificate and valid in all EU states and in countries that have adopted the
EU drone rules.

**Do I have to carry it when flying?** Yes, you must be able to show it if
checked.

**Does it allow me to fly alone with goggles?** No. In the open category you
always need an observer next to you when flying with goggles – see
[Where can I fly FPV in Germany?](/en/guides/where-to-fly-fpv-germany).
